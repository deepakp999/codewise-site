<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>The four stages of AI maturity and what each looks like in practice</li>

<li>A practical self-assessment you can apply to your own organization</li>

</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>Sarah (CEO) asked a deceptively simple question after the value mapping exercise: "So where are WE on this journey?"</p>
<p>Priya initially answered "early stage." But David pushed back: "Our supply chain forecasting system has been running for three years and saves us millions annually. That's not early stage."</p>
<p>They realized the answer depends on what you're measuring and which type of AI you're evaluating. Meridian was at different maturity levels for different capabilities — advanced in some areas, just beginning in others. Most organizations are the same. Maturity isn't a single score; it's a profile.</p>
<h2 id="the-four-stages-of-ai-maturity">The Four Stages of AI Maturity</h2>
<h3 id="stage-1-exploring">Stage 1: Exploring</h3>
<p><strong>What it looks like:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Leadership is learning about AI and discussing potential applications</li>
<li>Individual employees may be experimenting with AI tools</li>
<li>No formal AI strategy or budget allocation</li>
<li>Data infrastructure was built for reporting, not for AI</li>
<li>AI discussions are primarily about awareness and possibilities</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What Meridian found here:</strong> GenAI adoption. Individual teams used ChatGPT and Copilot informally, but there was no enterprise approach, no policy, and no measurement.</p>
<h3 id="stage-2-experimenting">Stage 2: Experimenting</h3>
<p><strong>What it looks like:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>A few pilot projects are underway, often driven by enthusiastic individuals or teams</li>
<li>Initial budget is allocated, typically within IT or a specific business unit</li>
<li>Data quality issues surface during pilot work</li>
<li>Early governance questions emerge (who approves AI use, what data is allowed)</li>
<li>Results from pilots are promising but not yet scaled</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What Meridian found here:</strong> Computer vision for quality control. The QC team had piloted defect detection on one production line with encouraging results, but it hadn't been expanded or integrated into broader operations.</p>
<h3 id="stage-3-scaling">Stage 3: Scaling</h3>
<p><strong>What it looks like:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>AI initiatives are tied to specific business KPIs and measured regularly</li>
<li>Multiple projects are in production across different departments</li>
<li>Data infrastructure has been upgraded to support AI workloads</li>
<li>Governance frameworks are in place</li>
<li>AI roles exist (data engineers, ML engineers, AI product managers)</li>
<li>The organization is learning how to operationalize AI — monitoring, retraining, managing model performance</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What Meridian found here:</strong> Supply chain demand forecasting and financial anomaly detection. These had been in production for years, with established data pipelines, regular model retraining, and clear business metrics.</p>
<h3 id="stage-4-transforming">Stage 4: Transforming</h3>
<p><strong>What it looks like:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>AI is embedded in core business processes and decision-making</li>
<li>The organization continuously identifies new AI opportunities</li>
<li>Data is treated as a strategic asset with enterprise-wide governance</li>
<li>AI capability is a competitive advantage and part of the company's identity</li>
<li>Leadership regularly reviews AI portfolio performance alongside other business metrics</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What Meridian found here:</strong> Not yet — and that's fine. Most organizations haven't reached this stage. According to Deloitte's 2026 survey, only 34% of organizations describe their AI impact as "truly transformative" — the majority are still in earlier stages (<a href="https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html">Deloitte 2026</a>).</p>

<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>This assessment is most valuable when completed collaboratively by the leadership team — each member sees different dimensions more clearly.</li>
<li>There's no "correct" stage. Being at Stage 1 is fine if you know it and plan accordingly. Being at Stage 3 on paper but Stage 1 in data readiness is a risk.</li>
<li>The assessment naturally surfaces the conversation about where to invest next — and that's exactly what Module 2 addresses.</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Overestimating maturity based on one success</strong> — Having a single AI project in production doesn't mean the organization is at "Scaling" maturity. Assessment should look across all five dimensions.</li>
<li><strong>Assessing only GenAI maturity</strong> — Many organizations are more mature in classical ML than they realize. Assessing both separately gives a more accurate picture.</li>
<li><strong>Treating maturity as a race</strong> — Not every organization needs to reach Stage 4. The right maturity level depends on your industry, competitive dynamics, and strategic priorities.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring data readiness</strong> — Data consistently emerges as the constraining dimension in maturity assessments. It deserves the most honest evaluation.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>AI maturity is a profile across five dimensions (strategy, data, talent, governance, technology), not a single score.</li>
<li>Assess maturity separately for classical ML and GenAI — most organizations have different levels for each.</li>
<li>Data readiness is the most common constraint, with fewer than one in five organizations reporting high maturity.</li>
<li>The lowest-scoring dimension typically constrains overall AI capability. Focus investment there first.</li>
<li>Module 1 is complete — you now have AI literacy: the landscape, the toolkit, how it works, how it creates value, and where you stand.</li>
</ul>
