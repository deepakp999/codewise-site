1b:T342e,<h1 id="your-first-90-days-as-an-ai-ready-leader">Your First 90 Days as an AI-Ready Leader</h1>
<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>A practical 90-day action plan adaptable to any role and organization</li>
<li>Specific activities by week, with clear outcomes</li>
<li>A one-page checklist you can use immediately</li>
<li>How to apply this regardless of where your organization is on AI adoption</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>After completing the leadership development program, Sarah (CEO) gathered the leadership team for a final session. "Now we need to convert what we've learned into action. Let's each commit to specific steps over the next 90 days — concrete enough that we can hold each other accountable."</p>
<p>The team developed individual 90-day plans tailored to their roles. The structure that emerged became Meridian's standard for AI literacy: assess in the first month, align in the second, apply in the third.</p>
<h2 id="the-90-day-framework">The 90-Day Framework</h2>
<h3 id="days-130-assess">Days 1–30: Assess</h3>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Understand where you and your organization actually stand.</p>
<p><strong>Week 1: Personal AI Inventory</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Take stock of your own AI literacy. Which lessons in this course were most challenging? Where do you want to deepen?</li>
<li>Identify two AI tools you'll use yourself over the next 90 days to build firsthand familiarity</li>
<li>Read one industry-specific AI report (Deloitte, McKinsey, or PwC quarterly publications)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 2: Organizational AI Inventory</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Working with IT, identify what AI tools and capabilities are currently in use across your team or organization</li>
<li>Note both formal initiatives and informal tool usage (the shadow AI conversation from Lesson 16)</li>
<li>Identify the data your team or organization generates that might be underutilized</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 3: Data Readiness Assessment</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>For your area of responsibility, complete the Data Readiness Scorecard from Lesson 8</li>
<li>Identify the top 3 data quality or availability issues that constrain your team's effectiveness</li>
<li>Discuss findings with relevant data owners</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 4: Maturity Assessment</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Complete the AI Maturity self-assessment from Lesson 5 for your area of responsibility</li>
<li>Compare your assessment with peer leaders' assessments</li>
<li>Identify the lowest-scoring dimension as your priority focus</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>End of Month 1 outcome:</strong> You have a clear, evidence-based picture of where AI literacy, AI usage, data readiness, and AI maturity stand for you and your area.</p>
<h3 id="days-3160-align">Days 31–60: Align</h3>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Build alignment around priorities and start meaningful action.</p>
<p><strong>Week 5: Connect to Business Priorities</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Map your organization's top 3 business priorities (from current strategy) to AI capabilities (from Lesson 2's full toolkit)</li>
<li>Identify 3–5 potential AI use cases that connect to those priorities</li>
<li>Apply the Opportunity Matrix from Lesson 7 to prioritize</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 6: Engage Cross-Functional Partners</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Schedule conversations with peers in IT, finance, legal, and HR about AI in your area</li>
<li>Share your assessment findings; learn theirs</li>
<li>Identify shared priorities and potential collaboration</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 7: Governance Engagement</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Understand or help establish your organization's AI governance approach</li>
<li>If formal governance doesn't yet exist, advocate for the lightweight model from Lesson 12</li>
<li>Ensure your team's AI usage is visible and appropriately governed</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 8: Strategy Document or Contribution</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>If your organization has an AI strategy: review it, identify how your area connects, and propose updates if needed</li>
<li>If not: contribute to building one using the template from Lesson 11</li>
<li>Even at the team level, document your area's AI approach</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>End of Month 2 outcome:</strong> Your AI priorities are aligned with business strategy, supported by cross-functional partners, and connected to organizational governance.</p>
<h3 id="days-6190-apply">Days 61–90: Apply</h3>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Move from planning to action — but in a way that's data-first and sustainable.</p>
<p><strong>Week 9: Address the Highest-Priority Data Gap</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>For your top-priority AI opportunity, address the most critical data readiness issue</li>
<li>This may mean integration work, quality improvement, governance, or all three</li>
<li>Don't try to fix everything — focus on what's needed for the priority use case</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 10: Initiate or Advance One AI Initiative</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Begin one concrete AI initiative within your area, sized to be achievable in a 90-day pilot</li>
<li>Apply the pilot-to-production thinking from Lesson 22 from the start</li>
<li>Define success metrics, baseline measurements, and the path to operational deployment</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 11: Develop Team Capability</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Identify 2–3 team members who would benefit from AI literacy development</li>
<li>Provide access to learning resources (this course, internal programs, vendor training)</li>
<li>Identify potential AI champions or translators on your team</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Week 12: Set Up Sustained Practice</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Establish a regular cadence for AI-related discussions in your team meetings (monthly is sufficient for most areas)</li>
<li>Schedule a quarterly review of your AI portfolio against business priorities</li>
<li>Plan for the next 90 days based on what you've learned</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>End of Month 3 outcome:</strong> You have meaningful AI activity underway, with sustainable practices for continued progress.</p>

<h2 id="adapting-to-your-context">Adapting to Your Context</h2>
<p>The 90-day plan adapts to your organizational stage:</p>
<p><strong>If your organization is at the Exploring stage:</strong> Focus on assessment and literacy in the first 60 days; one small pilot in days 61–90.</p>
<p><strong>If your organization is at the Experimenting stage:</strong> Use the plan to bring more rigor and alignment to existing activity; help establish governance and measurement practices.</p>
<p><strong>If your organization is at the Scaling stage:</strong> Use the plan to extend AI capability into new areas; focus on connecting your area to enterprise platforms and standards.</p>
<p><strong>If your organization is at the Transforming stage:</strong> Use the plan to identify next-horizon opportunities; advocate for emerging trends (Lesson 25) where relevant.</p>
<h2 id="a-note-on-sustained-progress">A Note on Sustained Progress</h2>
<p>Ninety days is enough to make meaningful progress. It's not enough to complete an AI transformation. The most important outcome of these 90 days is establishing sustained practices — regular review, cross-functional engagement, ongoing learning — that continue beyond the initial period.</p>
<p>The leaders who derive the most value from AI literacy aren't those who learn the most in 90 days. They're those who build practices that compound over years.</p>
<h2 id="final-thoughts">Final Thoughts</h2>
<p>Throughout this course, we've covered AI from many angles: the landscape, the toolkit, how it works, business value, maturity, strategy, data readiness, governance, risk, ethics, regulation, talent, change management, operating models, workforce, culture, pilots, ROI, scaling, and emerging trends.</p>
<p>The unifying principle: <strong>AI is a leadership topic, not just a technology topic.</strong> It touches every dimension of how organizations operate — strategy, people, processes, decisions, culture. Leaders who engage with AI thoughtfully — with clarity about what it can do, honesty about what it can't, and discipline in how they apply it — position their organizations for sustained success.</p>
<p>You don't need to become a technical AI expert. You do need to be informed enough to ask good questions, evaluate proposals, lead change, and make sound decisions. This course has given you that foundation.</p>
<p>The next 90 days are how you put it to work.</p>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Trying to do everything at once</strong> — The 90-day plan covers a lot. Focus on what's most relevant to your role and stage. Better to do fewer things well than many things superficially.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping the assessment phase</strong> — Action without assessment often produces activity without results. The first 30 days establish the foundation for the next 60.</li>
<li><strong>Working alone</strong> — AI is inherently cross-functional. Engaging peers in IT, finance, legal, and HR multiplies what you can accomplish.</li>
<li><strong>Treating 90 days as the end</strong> — The most important outcome is sustained practice. The 90-day plan starts a longer journey, not completes one.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>The 90-day plan structures action into three phases: Assess (Days 1–30), Align (Days 31–60), Apply (Days 61–90).</li>
<li>The most important outcome is sustained practice — regular review, cross-functional engagement, ongoing learning — that continues beyond the initial 90 days.</li>
<li>Adapt the plan to your organization's stage. Earlier-stage organizations focus more on assessment and literacy; later-stage organizations focus more on extending capability into new areas.</li>
<li>AI is a leadership topic. Engaging with it thoughtfully positions your organization for sustained success.</li>
<li>This course has given you the foundation. The next 90 days are how you put it to work.</li>
</ul>
