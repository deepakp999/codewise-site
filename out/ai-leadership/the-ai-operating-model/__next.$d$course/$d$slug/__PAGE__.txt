3:T276e,<h1 id="the-ai-operating-model--centralized-federated-or-hybrid">The AI Operating Model — Centralized, Federated, or Hybrid?</h1>
<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>The three primary AI operating model patterns</li>
<li>Trade-offs between each approach</li>
<li>How to choose the right model for your organization's stage</li>
<li>How operating models typically evolve</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>With talent strategy and change management approaches in place, Sarah (CEO) raised an organizational question: "Where does AI ownership sit in our org structure? Is this Priya's responsibility? Should each business unit own their AI initiatives? Should we create a new function?"</p>
<p>The team realized this wasn't just an org chart question — it would affect speed, consistency, and accountability for years to come.</p>
<h2 id="the-three-primary-models">The Three Primary Models</h2>
<h3 id="model-1-centralized-center-of-excellence">Model 1: Centralized (Center of Excellence)</h3>
<p>A single team owns AI capabilities and serves the rest of the organization.</p>
<pre><code>              CEO
               |
    ┌──────────┼──────────┐
    │          │          │
   CTO        CFO       Sales VP
    │
   AI Center of Excellence
   (Data engineers, ML engineers,
    data scientists, AI PMs)
    │
    └─→ Serves all business units
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Consistent standards, governance, and quality</li>
<li>Efficient use of scarce specialist talent</li>
<li>Easier to manage data and infrastructure investment</li>
<li>Clear accountability for AI decisions</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Can become a bottleneck as demand grows</li>
<li>Distance from business context may slow use case development</li>
<li>Business units may feel disconnected from "their" AI initiatives</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>When it fits:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Early-stage AI maturity (Exploring or Experimenting)</li>
<li>Limited specialist talent</li>
<li>Strong need for consistent governance and standards</li>
<li>Smaller organizations where centralization is naturally efficient</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="model-2-federated-embedded-in-business-units">Model 2: Federated (Embedded in Business Units)</h3>
<p>Each business unit has its own AI capability, with light coordination across units.</p>
<pre><code>              CEO
               |
    ┌──────────┼──────────┐
    │          │          │
  Manufacturing  Finance  Sales
    │          │          │
   AI Team    AI Team    AI Team
   (their own)(their own)(their own)
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Close to business context, faster use case identification</li>
<li>Business units feel ownership of their AI work</li>
<li>Natural alignment with business priorities</li>
<li>Speed of execution within each unit</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Inconsistent standards and governance across units</li>
<li>Duplicated effort and infrastructure investment</li>
<li>Difficulty sharing learning across units</li>
<li>Can create competing or incompatible approaches</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>When it fits:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Mature AI organizations</li>
<li>Highly diverse business units with very different needs</li>
<li>Organizations where business unit autonomy is a strong cultural value</li>
<li>Companies with significant scale that can support specialist talent in each unit</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="model-3-hybrid-hub-and-spoke">Model 3: Hybrid (Hub and Spoke)</h3>
<p>A central team provides shared capabilities (platform, standards, specialists) while business units have embedded translators and AI champions.</p>
<pre><code>              CEO
               |
    ┌──────────┼──────────┐
    │          │          │
   CTO        CFO        Sales VP
    │          │          │
  AI Hub     AI lead in   AI lead in
  (Platform, finance     sales
  specialists, (translator) (translator)
  governance)
    │      ←→  Coordinated through AI Hub
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Combines centralized expertise with business proximity</li>
<li>Shared platforms and standards reduce duplication</li>
<li>Business units retain ownership of their use cases</li>
<li>Specialist talent is leveraged across the organization</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>More complex to set up and govern</li>
<li>Requires clear definition of central vs distributed responsibilities</li>
<li>Coordination overhead</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>When it fits:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Most mid-to-large organizations as they mature</li>
<li>Organizations where both consistency and business proximity matter</li>
<li>Companies with diverse business units that can benefit from shared platforms</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-operating-models-evolve">How Operating Models Evolve</h2>
<p>Most organizations follow a maturity progression:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Stage</th>
<th>Typical Model</th>
<th>Reason</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Exploring (Stage 1)</td>
<td>No formal model</td>
<td>Individual experimentation</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Experimenting (Stage 2)</td>
<td>Centralized</td>
<td>Concentrate talent, learn together</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Scaling (Stage 3)</td>
<td>Hybrid</td>
<td>Combine central platform with business proximity</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Transforming (Stage 4)</td>
<td>Hybrid or Federated</td>
<td>AI capability embedded throughout</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>There's no single "right" model — the right model depends on stage, organizational structure, and culture.</p>
<h2 id="meridians-operating-model-decision">Meridian's Operating Model Decision</h2>
<p>Meridian's leadership chose a hybrid model:</p>
<p><strong>Central AI Hub (reporting to CTO):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Data platform and infrastructure</li>
<li>Specialist talent (data engineers, ML engineers)</li>
<li>Governance framework and review process</li>
<li>Standards and shared tooling</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Embedded AI Champions in business units:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Identify AI use cases relevant to their domain</li>
<li>Coordinate with the central hub on implementation</li>
<li>Drive adoption within their teams</li>
<li>Provide business context for prioritization</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Cross-functional AI Governance Committee:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reviews and approves new initiatives</li>
<li>Includes leaders from technology, finance, legal, HR, and rotating business unit representatives</li>
<li>Meets monthly</li>
</ul>
<p>The hybrid model gave Meridian centralized governance and shared platforms while keeping business units close to use case identification.</p>
<h2 id="budget-and-accountability">Budget and Accountability</h2>
<p>Operating model choice directly affects budgeting:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Model</th>
<th>Budget Pattern</th>
<th>Accountability</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Centralized</td>
<td>Central AI budget, business units request services</td>
<td>Central team accountable for delivery</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Federated</td>
<td>Each business unit funds and owns their AI</td>
<td>Business units accountable for results</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Hybrid</td>
<td>Shared platform budget centrally, use case budgets in business units</td>
<td>Joint accountability — central for platform, business for outcomes</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Defining how AI investment is funded clarifies who owns what and reduces conflict over priorities.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>Operating model is a significant decision with multi-year implications. Take time to consider trade-offs with the leadership team.</li>
<li>The right model depends on your stage, structure, and culture — not on what other organizations are doing.</li>
<li>Plan to evolve. The model that fits at the Experimenting stage may not fit at the Scaling stage.</li>
<li>Whatever model you choose, define clearly: who owns what, how decisions are made, how budget flows, and how coordination happens.</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Choosing federated too early</strong> — Federated models work when AI maturity is high across business units. Adopting federated at the Experimenting stage typically produces inconsistent results and duplicated effort.</li>
<li><strong>Centralizing forever</strong> — Centralized models can become bottlenecks as demand grows. Plan to evolve toward hybrid or federated as the organization matures.</li>
<li><strong>Hybrid in name only</strong> — A hybrid model that doesn't clearly define central vs distributed responsibilities becomes ambiguous and slow.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring budget structure</strong> — Operating model and budget structure should align. Mismatched structures create friction and unclear accountability.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Three primary operating models: centralized (Center of Excellence), federated (embedded in business units), hybrid (hub and spoke).</li>
<li>Most organizations evolve from centralized → hybrid as they mature. Federated typically emerges only at advanced maturity.</li>
<li>The right model depends on stage, structure, and culture — there's no universal answer.</li>
<li>Operating model affects budgeting, accountability, and speed. Choose deliberately.</li>
<li>Plan to evolve as your organization's AI maturity grows.</li>
</ul>
