1b:T24c9,<h1 id="build-buy-or-partner--making-the-right-ai-investment-decisions">Build, Buy, or Partner — Making the Right AI Investment Decisions</h1>
<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>When to build custom AI, when to buy SaaS, when to partner</li>
<li>The four-factor decision framework: Differentiation, Data, Cost, Speed</li>
<li>How data readiness shapes the build-vs-buy decision</li>
<li>Practical evaluation criteria for AI vendors</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>With use cases prioritized and data foundations underway, Priya (CTO) presented three options for the invoice processing initiative:</p>
<p><strong>Option A (Build):</strong> Hire ML engineers, build a custom model trained on Meridian's invoice formats. Timeline: 6–9 months.</p>
<p><strong>Option B (Buy):</strong> Subscribe to a commercial document processing platform. Timeline: 6–8 weeks.</p>
<p><strong>Option C (Partner):</strong> Engage a specialized firm to build a tailored solution deployed on Meridian's infrastructure. Timeline: 3–4 months.</p>
<p>David (CFO) asked the right question: "What's the framework? This isn't just a cost comparison." He was right — the answer depends on strategic context, not just price.</p>
<h2 id="the-four-factor-framework">The Four-Factor Framework</h2>
<h3 id="factor-1-strategic-differentiation">Factor 1: Strategic Differentiation</h3>
<p>Is this capability a competitive advantage or a commodity?</p>
<p>Invoice processing is operational efficiency — valuable, but not unique to Meridian. Buying makes sense. Meridian's demand forecasting, built on proprietary supply chain data and customer patterns, is potentially a differentiator. Building makes more sense.</p>
<p><strong>Low differentiation → lean toward BUY.</strong> High differentiation → lean toward BUILD.</p>
<h3 id="factor-2-data-sensitivity-and-readiness">Factor 2: Data Sensitivity and Readiness</h3>
<p>Where does your data go, and is it AI-ready?</p>
<p>SaaS tools process data in the vendor's cloud. For regulated industries, this raises compliance questions. Building keeps data on your infrastructure.</p>
<p>Data readiness also affects the decision: building custom AI requires clean, sufficient, well-structured data. If your data isn't there yet, a SaaS tool that works with less data preparation may deliver value faster while you build foundations.</p>
<p><strong>Sensitive/regulated data → lean toward BUILD or on-premise PARTNER.</strong> Data not yet AI-ready → lean toward BUY (for now).</p>

<h3 id="factor-4-speed-to-value">Factor 4: Speed to Value</h3>
<p>How quickly does the organization need results?</p>
<p>Building takes months to years. Buying can deliver value in weeks. Partnering falls in between.</p>
<p>For Meridian's invoice processing (operational efficiency, no competitive differentiation, data is structured and available), the answer was clear: <strong>Buy.</strong> Time-to-value mattered more than customization, and the capability wasn't a strategic differentiator.</p>
<p>For demand forecasting (competitive advantage, proprietary data, existing ML expertise): <strong>Build</strong>, with the option to partner for initial acceleration.</p>

<h2 id="evaluating-ai-vendors-when-you-buy-or-partner">Evaluating AI Vendors (When You Buy or Partner)</h2>
<p>When evaluating vendors, consider these areas:</p>
<p><strong>Technical fit:</strong> Does the solution address your specific use case? Can it integrate with your existing systems? What's the implementation complexity?</p>
<p><strong>Data handling:</strong> Where is data processed and stored? What security and privacy controls are in place? Can you retrieve your data if you switch vendors?</p>
<p><strong>Transparency:</strong> Can the vendor explain how their models work? What's the accuracy on data similar to yours (not just their best benchmark)? How do they handle errors and edge cases?</p>
<p><strong>Track record:</strong> Do they have customers in your industry? Can they provide reference cases with measurable outcomes?</p>
<p><strong>Roadmap alignment:</strong> Is the vendor investing in capabilities you'll need in 2–3 years? Are they dependent on a single model provider that could change terms?</p>
<p><strong>Commercial terms:</strong> What happens if you want to leave? Is there a data portability clause? How does pricing scale with volume?</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>Most organizations will use a mix: buy for commodity capabilities, build for differentiators, partner for initial acceleration.</li>
<li>Data readiness shapes this decision more than most leaders realize. If your data isn't ready, building custom AI will stall — buying a SaaS tool that works with less preparation may be the pragmatic first step.</li>
<li>Revisit build-vs-buy decisions as your organization matures. What you buy today (because you lack internal capability) you might build in-house next year (once you've developed the team and data foundations).</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Building everything because "we want to own the IP"</strong> — Owning custom AI is only valuable if the capability is a differentiator. Building a custom email classification system when a commercial tool does the same thing is a misallocation of scarce AI talent.</li>
<li><strong>Buying without evaluating data portability</strong> — If you can't export your data and models when a vendor relationship ends, you're locked in. Always negotiate data portability.</li>
<li><strong>Assuming a vendor's demo reflects your reality</strong> — Vendor demos use clean, curated data. Ask for performance metrics on data similar to yours, or request a proof-of-concept on your actual data.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring total cost of ownership</strong> — A $50K/year SaaS tool processing 10 million documents costs $5 per thousand. At Meridian's scale, that's reasonable. At 10x scale, building might be cheaper.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Build when AI is a competitive differentiator and you have the data and talent. Buy when the capability is a commodity and speed matters. Partner when you need expertise to get started.</li>
<li>Data readiness is a decisive factor — it affects not just whether AI works, but which approach is practical.</li>
<li>Most organizations use a mix of all three approaches, evolving over time as internal capabilities grow.</li>
<li>When buying, evaluate data handling, transparency, portability, and total cost of ownership — not just features and price.</li>
</ul>
