3:T2a47,<h1 id="scaling-ai-across-the-enterprise--from-one-team-to-every-team">Scaling AI Across the Enterprise — From One Team to Every Team</h1>
<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>Why scaling AI is different from launching AI</li>
<li>The scaling playbook: platforms, reusable components, self-service</li>
<li>The 80/20 rule: why technology is only part of the story</li>
<li>How data products enable scaling efficiency</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>With invoice processing in production and demand forecasting expansion underway, the leadership team asked a natural next question: how do we go from two AI successes to ten? To twenty?</p>
<p>Priya (CTO) observed: "The first AI initiative is the hardest. The second should be easier if we've built foundations right. By the fifth, we should be significantly faster. If we're not — if each initiative takes as much effort as the first — we haven't built a scaling capability, just a project portfolio."</p>
<h2 id="why-scaling-is-different">Why Scaling Is Different</h2>
<p>Scaling AI isn't just about doing more AI. It's about building capability that makes each successive initiative faster, cheaper, and more reliable than the previous one.</p>
<p>Key differences between launching AI and scaling AI:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Launching (First 1–2 Initiatives)</th>
<th>Scaling (Initiatives 3–10+)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Build everything custom</td>
<td>Leverage shared platforms</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Unique data pipeline per use case</td>
<td>Reusable data products</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Bespoke monitoring and governance</td>
<td>Standardized operations framework</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Learning-intensive</td>
<td>Pattern-applying</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>High per-initiative investment</td>
<td>Decreasing marginal cost</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Organizations that scale successfully invest in the foundations that make future initiatives efficient. Organizations that don't scale treat each initiative as independent, paying the full startup cost every time.</p>
<h2 id="the-scaling-playbook">The Scaling Playbook</h2>
<h3 id="element-1-standardized-platforms">Element 1: Standardized Platforms</h3>
<p>Rather than each AI initiative building its own infrastructure, invest in shared platforms:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Data platform</strong> — centralized data warehouse or lake, integrated pipelines, quality monitoring</li>
<li><strong>ML platform</strong> — infrastructure for training, deploying, and monitoring models</li>
<li><strong>GenAI platform</strong> — centralized access to LLMs with appropriate governance, prompt management, usage tracking</li>
<li><strong>Observability platform</strong> — standardized monitoring, alerting, and incident response</li>
</ul>
<p>The platform investment is substantial but compounds. The second initiative using a platform costs a fraction of the first. By the fifth or tenth initiative, platform ROI is clear.</p>
<h3 id="element-2-reusable-data-products">Element 2: Reusable Data Products</h3>
<p>The data product mindset (Lesson 9) is essential for scaling. When clean, governed datasets exist as managed products:</p>
<ul>
<li>A "Customer 360" data product serves the churn prediction model, the recommendation engine, the customer support AI, and the marketing personalization tool</li>
<li>A "Product Catalog" data product feeds pricing optimization, inventory management, and search personalization</li>
<li>A "Sales Transactions" data product supports forecasting, analytics, and revenue attribution</li>
</ul>
<p>Building a data product once and reusing it many times is dramatically more efficient than rebuilding data pipelines for each AI initiative.</p>
<h3 id="element-3-self-service-capability">Element 3: Self-Service Capability</h3>
<p>As AI maturity grows, more of the work should shift from specialists to business users with specialist support:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Business analysts</strong> should be able to build simple models and dashboards using approved tools and curated data</li>
<li><strong>Domain experts</strong> should be able to apply pre-built AI capabilities to their workflows</li>
<li><strong>AI champions</strong> in each business unit should be able to assess opportunities and coordinate implementation</li>
</ul>
<p>Self-service doesn't mean no specialists. It means specialists focus on the hard, novel problems while routine AI application is distributed.</p>
<h3 id="element-4-embedded-ai-in-workflows">Element 4: Embedded AI in Workflows</h3>
<p>Scaling means AI becomes part of how work happens, not a separate tool people have to remember to use. Integration into:</p>
<ul>
<li>Existing business systems (ERP, CRM, collaboration platforms)</li>
<li>Standard workflows and processes</li>
<li>Decision templates and operating rhythms</li>
<li>Reporting and dashboards</li>
</ul>
<p>When AI is embedded, adoption doesn't require sustained campaigning — the AI is simply present where the work happens.</p>
<h2 id="the-8020-rule">The 80/20 Rule</h2>
<p>PwC's 2026 AI predictions include a useful observation: technology typically delivers about 20% of an AI initiative's value. The other 80% comes from redesigning how work happens (<a href="https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-predictions.html">PwC 2026</a>).</p>
<p>This has direct implications for scaling:</p>
<ul>
<li>If scaling efforts focus primarily on technology (more models, more tools, bigger platforms), they'll capture only the 20% portion of potential value</li>
<li>The larger opportunity is in redesigning workflows, decision processes, and operating rhythms to take full advantage of AI capabilities</li>
<li>Change management and role evolution (Modules 4) are essential components of scaling, not supporting activities</li>
</ul>
<p>Organizations that understand the 80/20 ratio allocate resources accordingly — typically more to change management, training, and workflow redesign than most initial plans account for.</p>
<h2 id="scaling-readiness-indicators">Scaling Readiness Indicators</h2>
<p>How do you know your organization is ready to scale?</p>
<p><strong>Signs of scaling readiness:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>First AI initiatives are in stable production with sustained results</li>
<li>Shared platforms exist for data, ML, and/or GenAI</li>
<li>Data products are defined and maintained for key domains</li>
<li>Governance framework handles routine initiatives without bottleneck</li>
<li>Cross-functional coordination is working (governance committee, operating model)</li>
<li>Leaders across the organization have basic AI literacy</li>
<li>There's a pipeline of business-identified use cases, not just technology-driven ideas</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Signs of scaling risk:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Each new AI initiative takes the same effort as the first</li>
<li>Data pipelines are built per use case with no reuse</li>
<li>Governance reviews are bottlenecks that slow initiatives</li>
<li>Business units don't know what's possible or how to engage</li>
<li>Specialists are the only ones who can deliver AI value</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="meridians-scaling-approach">Meridian's Scaling Approach</h2>
<p>With two initiatives in production, Meridian's 12-month scaling plan:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Platform investment</strong> — Formalize the data platform work (Snowflake + pipelines) as the shared AI data foundation</li>
<li><strong>Data products</strong> — Designate "Customer 360," "Product Catalog," and "Sales Transactions" as managed data products with owners and SLAs</li>
<li><strong>Use case pipeline</strong> — Quarterly reviews in each business unit to identify new use cases, prioritized through the Opportunity Matrix</li>
<li><strong>Governance efficiency</strong> — Streamline the governance review process for low-risk initiatives so routine cases don't require full committee review</li>
<li><strong>Literacy expansion</strong> — Extend AI literacy programs to 200+ managers and senior contributors beyond the initial 45 directors</li>
<li><strong>Workflow integration</strong> — For each production AI capability, map the workflows where it should be embedded and plan integration work</li>
</ol>
<p>The goal: by the end of Year 2, have 6–10 AI capabilities in production, with decreasing per-initiative cost and a pipeline of well-prioritized opportunities for Year 3.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>Scaling AI is a different challenge than launching AI. Plan for platform investment, data products, self-service capability, and workflow integration.</li>
<li>The 80/20 rule matters: if you're allocating 95% to technology and 5% to change and workflow redesign, the ratio is off.</li>
<li>Data products are the single highest-leverage scaling investment. Build them deliberately.</li>
<li>Scaling success is measured by decreasing marginal cost and time per initiative, not just by the number of initiatives.</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Treating scaling as "more of the same"</strong> — If each new initiative starts from scratch, you're launching ten first-time initiatives, not scaling.</li>
<li><strong>Underinvesting in platforms</strong> — Shared platforms feel expensive upfront but reduce per-initiative cost dramatically. Without them, each initiative pays full startup costs.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring the 80/20 ratio</strong> — Scaling efforts that focus only on technology miss most of the potential value.</li>
<li><strong>Scaling before foundations are stable</strong> — Scaling amplifies both what works and what doesn't. Unstable foundations scale into bigger problems.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Scaling is different from launching. It requires platforms, reusable components, self-service capability, and workflow integration.</li>
<li>PwC's 80/20 rule: technology delivers ~20% of AI value; work redesign delivers the other 80%. Plan resource allocation accordingly.</li>
<li>Data products are the highest-leverage scaling investment — one clean dataset can serve many AI initiatives.</li>
<li>Scaling success means decreasing marginal cost per initiative, not just more initiatives.</li>
<li>Scaling readiness includes stable production initiatives, working governance, cross-functional coordination, and broad AI literacy.</li>
</ul>
