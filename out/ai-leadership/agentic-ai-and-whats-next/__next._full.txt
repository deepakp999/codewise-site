1b:T29a7,<h1 id="agentic-ai-sovereign-ai-and-whats-coming--a-leaders-horizon-scan">Agentic AI, Sovereign AI, and What's Coming — A Leader's Horizon Scan</h1>
<div data-cw-section="learn-box"><h2 id="what-youll-learn">What You'll Learn</h2>
<ul>
<li>The four emerging AI trends that matter most for enterprise strategy</li>
<li>What's hype, what's real, and what's between</li>
<li>How each trend amplifies the importance of data readiness</li>
<li>Strategic implications for the next 2–3 years</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>In a strategic planning session, Sarah (CEO) asked the team a forward-looking question: "What should we be watching that isn't on our current roadmap? What might be table stakes in two years that we'd want to be prepared for?"</p>
<p>This kind of horizon scanning isn't speculation — it's strategic preparation. Capabilities that seem distant today often become operational expectations faster than expected. Leaders who track emerging trends can position their organizations to adapt rather than react.</p>
<h2 id="trend-1-agentic-ai">Trend 1: Agentic AI</h2>
<p><strong>What it is:</strong> AI systems that can plan and execute multi-step tasks with a degree of autonomy. Instead of answering a question, an agent might research a topic, draft a document, schedule meetings, send communications, and follow up — all from a single high-level instruction.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stands today:</strong> Early enterprise adoption. Agentic capabilities exist in commercial tools, but production-grade enterprise deployment is still maturing. PwC's 2026 predictions identify agentic workflows as a significant emerging opportunity, with the caveat that organizations need clear governance and human oversight (<a href="https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-predictions.html">PwC 2026</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Strategic implications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Agents can handle workflows that span multiple systems and require sequential decisions — areas previously difficult to automate</li>
<li>The data demands for agentic systems are even higher than for supervised AI: agents need real-time, accurate, well-governed data to make autonomous decisions</li>
<li>Governance becomes more critical, not less. Autonomous action requires robust oversight frameworks</li>
<li>Roles evolve: humans shift from doing the work to designing and supervising agent workflows</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to watch:</strong> Track how peers in your industry are piloting agentic capabilities. Identify 1–2 workflows where agentic AI might be valuable — typically multi-step processes that span systems and require coordination.</p>
<h2 id="trend-2-multimodal-ai">Trend 2: Multimodal AI</h2>
<p><strong>What it is:</strong> AI systems that work across multiple types of input and output — text, images, audio, video, structured data — in unified ways. A multimodal system might analyze a video, generate a written summary, create accompanying charts, and answer follow-up questions about all of it.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stands today:</strong> Rapidly maturing. Major AI providers offer multimodal capabilities. Enterprise applications are emerging, particularly in fields involving rich media (manufacturing, healthcare imaging, media, retail).</p>
<p><strong>Strategic implications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Many enterprise data sources have been underutilized because they're in non-text formats: meeting recordings, security camera footage, manufacturing imagery, training videos</li>
<li>Multimodal capabilities can unlock value from these previously hard-to-analyze sources</li>
<li>Data infrastructure expands to include unstructured media, not just text and structured data</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to watch:</strong> Identify high-volume non-text data your organization generates that might benefit from multimodal analysis (call center recordings, video communications, visual records).</p>
<h2 id="trend-3-sovereign-ai">Trend 3: Sovereign AI</h2>
<p><strong>What it is:</strong> AI capabilities that organizations or governments control end-to-end — running on their infrastructure, using models they control, with data that doesn't leave their environment. The driver: data sovereignty, regulatory compliance, and reduced vendor dependency.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stands today:</strong> Growing interest, particularly in regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, government) and in regions with strong data sovereignty regulations. Open-source models have made sovereign AI more practical than it was 2 years ago.</p>
<p><strong>Strategic implications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>For regulated industries or sensitive use cases, sovereign approaches reduce compliance and dependency risks</li>
<li>Open-source models combined with private cloud or on-premise infrastructure offer alternatives to public AI APIs</li>
<li>Trade-off: sovereign AI typically requires more internal capability to deploy and maintain</li>
<li>Hybrid approaches (sovereign for sensitive use cases, commercial APIs for others) are common</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to watch:</strong> Assess which of your AI use cases would benefit from sovereign deployment based on data sensitivity, regulatory requirements, and strategic importance.</p>
<h2 id="trend-4-physical-ai">Trend 4: Physical AI</h2>
<p><strong>What it is:</strong> AI integrated with physical systems — robotics, autonomous vehicles, smart manufacturing, IoT-enabled operations. AI capabilities extend beyond information processing into physical action.</p>
<p><strong>Where it stands today:</strong> Maturing rapidly in specific domains. Manufacturing automation, warehouse robotics, autonomous vehicles in controlled environments are operational. General-purpose physical AI is still developing.</p>
<p><strong>Strategic implications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>For organizations with physical operations (manufacturing, logistics, field services), physical AI represents significant operational opportunities</li>
<li>Integration of digital and physical AI requires investment in sensors, connectivity, and data infrastructure</li>
<li>Safety and reliability requirements are different from purely digital AI</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to watch:</strong> For organizations with physical operations, identify where AI-enabled physical systems could improve safety, quality, throughput, or cost.</p>
<h2 id="how-these-trends-amplify-data-readiness">How These Trends Amplify Data Readiness</h2>
<p>A consistent pattern across all four trends: data readiness becomes more important, not less.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Agentic AI</strong> needs real-time, accurate data to make autonomous decisions</li>
<li><strong>Multimodal AI</strong> expands data infrastructure to handle rich media</li>
<li><strong>Sovereign AI</strong> requires complete data control end-to-end</li>
<li><strong>Physical AI</strong> generates and consumes massive volumes of operational data</li>
</ul>
<p>The data foundations built today (Lessons 8–9) become more valuable as these trends mature. Organizations with strong data readiness will adopt these capabilities faster and more reliably than those without.</p>
<h2 id="whats-hype-whats-real">What's Hype, What's Real</h2>
<p>It's useful to apply healthy skepticism to emerging trend coverage:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Often Overhyped</th>
<th>Often Underappreciated</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>"AI will replace [profession] within X years"</td>
<td>Compounding value of foundational work</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Specific timeline predictions</td>
<td>Importance of organizational adoption capability</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>AI capabilities demonstrated in narrow demos</td>
<td>Engineering effort required for production reliability</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Universal applicability claims</td>
<td>Domain-specific advantages</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A practical approach: track multiple credible sources, focus on what enterprises are actually deploying (not what's being demonstrated), and weight peer industry adoption more heavily than vendor projections.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>Horizon scanning is part of strategic leadership, not a separate activity. Allocate time quarterly to track emerging trends.</li>
<li>Avoid both extremes: dismissing emerging capabilities as hype, or rushing to adopt before they're production-ready for your context.</li>
<li>Foundational investments — data readiness, governance, talent literacy — make adoption of any emerging capability faster and more reliable.</li>
<li>Identify 1–2 trends most relevant to your industry and operations, and track them more deeply.</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chasing every emerging trend</strong> — Most organizations can't pursue everything. Pick the trends most relevant to your strategic context and ignore the rest until they mature.</li>
<li><strong>Dismissing trends as hype</strong> — The opposite extreme. Trends that seem distant today often become operational expectations faster than expected.</li>
<li><strong>Underestimating data readiness requirements</strong> — Each emerging trend has higher data requirements than current AI. Foundational data work pays off across all of them.</li>
<li><strong>Focusing on capability without considering adoption</strong> — A new AI capability is only valuable if it can be adopted. Organizational adoption capability often constrains what's possible.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Four trends matter most for enterprise strategy: agentic AI, multimodal AI, sovereign AI, and physical AI.</li>
<li>Each trend has higher data readiness requirements than current AI applications. Foundational data work compounds in value as these trends mature.</li>
<li>Apply healthy skepticism to trend coverage: focus on what enterprises are actually deploying, not what's being demonstrated.</li>
<li>Identify 1–2 trends most relevant to your context for deeper tracking; for the rest, monitor at a high level.</li>
<li>Foundational investments — data, governance, literacy — position the organization to adopt any of these trends faster than starting from scratch.</li>
</ul>
