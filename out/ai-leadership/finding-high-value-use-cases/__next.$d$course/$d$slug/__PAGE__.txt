</div><h2 id="the-meridian-story">The Meridian Story</h2>
<p>With the distinction between activity and strategy clear, Sarah (CEO) asked the team to brainstorm: "Where could AI create the most value at Meridian?"</p>
<p>In 90 minutes, the leadership team generated 28 potential use cases — from predictive maintenance on manufacturing equipment to GenAI-powered customer support to automated compliance monitoring. The energy was high.</p>
<p>Then David (CFO) asked his characteristic question: "We can't fund 28 initiatives. How do we pick 3?"</p>
<p>Priya (CTO) introduced the Opportunity Matrix — a framework she'd adapted from her experience at a previous organization. The idea: evaluate each use case on two dimensions, then focus resources where the intersection is strongest.</p>
<h2 id="the-opportunity-matrix">The Opportunity Matrix</h2>
<p>Plot each potential use case on two axes:</p>
<p><strong>Y-Axis: Business Impact</strong> — How much value does this create if it works?</p>
<p>Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Revenue potential (new revenue or protected revenue)</li>
<li>Cost savings (operational efficiency, reduced waste, automation)</li>
<li>Risk reduction (compliance, fraud prevention, safety)</li>
<li>Strategic importance (competitive differentiation, customer experience)</li>
<li>Scale of affected operations (touches 10 people or 10,000?)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>X-Axis: Feasibility</strong> — How realistic is successful implementation?</p>
<p>Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Data readiness</strong> (is the required data available, clean, and accessible?)</li>
<li>Technical complexity (off-the-shelf solution vs custom development?)</li>
<li>Organizational readiness (does the team have skills and willingness?)</li>
<li>Time to value (months or years?)</li>
<li>Regulatory constraints (does this use case involve sensitive data or regulated decisions?)</li>
</ul>
<pre><code>                    HIGH IMPACT
                        │
         ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
         │  STRATEGIC    │  START HERE  │
         │  BETS         │  (Quick Wins │
         │  (High value, │   + High     │
         │  needs        │   Impact)    │

                        │
                    LOW IMPACT
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Start Here (High Impact + High Feasibility):</strong> These are your first investments. The data is available, the technology is proven, and the business impact is clear.</p>
<p><strong>Easy Wins (Low Impact + High Feasibility):</strong> Quick to implement, useful for building organizational confidence with AI, but don't bet the strategy on them.</p>
<p><strong>Strategic Bets (High Impact + Low Feasibility):</strong> Worth pursuing, but need investment in data, talent, or technology first. These often become "Start Here" opportunities after foundational work.</p>
<p><strong>Deprioritize (Low Impact + Low Feasibility):</strong> Not worth the effort right now. Revisit later as conditions change.</p>
<h2 id="data-readiness-as-the-decisive-feasibility-factor">Data Readiness as the Decisive Feasibility Factor</h2>
<p>Of all feasibility considerations, data readiness deserves special weight. A high-impact use case with excellent data is a strong investment. A high-impact use case with poor data quality, fragmented sources, and no governance is a project that will stall — regardless of how good the AI technology is.</p>
<p>When scoring feasibility, consider:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Is the data available?</strong> Does it exist in a system you can access?</li>
<li><strong>Is the data clean?</strong> Accurate, complete, consistent, timely?</li>
<li><strong>Is the data integrated?</strong> Or does it live in separate systems that don't talk to each other?</li>
<li><strong>Is the data governed?</strong> Do you know who owns it, who can access it, and what quality standards apply?</li>
<li><strong>Is the data sufficient?</strong> Is there enough historical data to train or evaluate a model?</li>
</ul>
<p>A practical rule: <strong>never rate a use case as "high feasibility" if the underlying data scores low on any of these dimensions.</strong> The best AI model in the world cannot compensate for unreliable data.</p>

<h2 id="the-use-case-discovery-process">The Use Case Discovery Process</h2>
<p>How to generate the initial long list:</p>
<p><strong>1. Business-back approach:</strong> Start from the three-year strategic plan. For each priority, ask: "Where could better predictions, faster processing, or automated decisions accelerate this goal?"</p>
<p><strong>2. Pain-point approach:</strong> Ask each department: "What are the most time-consuming, repetitive, or error-prone processes in your area?" These often map directly to AI opportunities.</p>
<p><strong>3. Data-forward approach:</strong> Ask the data team: "Where do we have the richest, cleanest data?" Sometimes the best use case isn't the most obvious one — it's the one where data readiness is highest.</p>
<p><strong>4. Benchmark approach:</strong> Research what peer organizations in your industry have implemented successfully. Industry-specific AI use case libraries from Deloitte, McKinsey, and Gartner can provide structured starting points.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-your-organization">What This Means for Your Organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use the Opportunity Matrix as a collaborative exercise with your leadership team. Each member scores differently based on their domain expertise — that diversity of perspective improves prioritization quality.</li>
<li>Limit your first wave to 2–3 initiatives. Concentration of resources is more important than breadth.</li>
<li>Include at least one "Easy Win" in the first wave — organizational confidence grows from visible success.</li>
</ul>
<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prioritizing based on excitement rather than feasibility</strong> — The most exciting use case is often the hardest to implement. Balance ambition with readiness.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring data readiness in feasibility scoring</strong> — This is the most common reason AI pilots stall. Lesson 8 provides a detailed data readiness assessment framework.</li>
<li><strong>Starting with too many initiatives</strong> — Focus creates momentum. Spreading resources across 10 pilots creates 10 slow-moving experiments instead of 2–3 meaningful results.</li>
<li><strong>Only considering GenAI use cases</strong> — The full AI toolkit (Lesson 2) often reveals classical ML opportunities with higher feasibility and faster payback.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>The Opportunity Matrix (Impact vs Feasibility) is a practical tool for narrowing many AI ideas to a few high-confidence investments.</li>
<li>Data readiness is the single most important factor in feasibility assessment. Strong data enables everything; poor data stalls everything.</li>
<li>Start with 2–3 initiatives: at least one "Start Here" (high impact + high feasibility) and one "Easy Win" (builds confidence).</li>
<li>Use multiple discovery approaches — business-back, pain-points, data-forward, and benchmarks — to generate a comprehensive list before prioritizing.</li>
</ul>
