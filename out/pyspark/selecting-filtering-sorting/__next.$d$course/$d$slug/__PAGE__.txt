<div data-cw-section="mistake-card"><h2 id="common-mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Forgetting parentheses in compound conditions.</strong> <code>col("a") > 1 &#x26; col("b") &#x3C; 5</code> will break because <code>&#x26;</code> has higher precedence than <code>></code>. Always wrap each condition: <code>(col("a") > 1) &#x26; (col("b") &#x3C; 5)</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Using Python's <code>and</code>/<code>or</code> instead of <code>&#x26;</code>/<code>|</code>.</strong> Python's <code>and</code> and <code>or</code> don't work with PySpark column expressions. Use <code>&#x26;</code> for AND, <code>|</code> for OR, <code>~</code> for NOT.</li>
<li><strong>Assuming <code>orderBy</code> is free.</strong> Sorting requires a shuffle — Spark has to compare values across all partitions. On large datasets, an unnecessary <code>orderBy</code> can be the slowest part of your pipeline. Only sort when you actually need ordered output.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>select()</code> picks columns, <code>filter()</code>/<code>where()</code> picks rows, <code>orderBy()</code>/<code>sort()</code> orders results.</li>
<li><code>filter()</code> and <code>where()</code> are identical — pick one and be consistent.</li>
<li>Use <code>&#x26;</code>, <code>|</code>, <code>~</code> for combining conditions, with parentheses around each condition.</li>
<li>Use <code>isin()</code> instead of chaining multiple OR conditions.</li>
<li>Operations chain naturally: <code>df.filter(...).select(...).orderBy(...)</code>.</li>
<li>Sorting triggers a shuffle — don't sort unless you need to.</li>
</ul>
